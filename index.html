The points

What the report is actually useful for

These are the hooks in the CNA story. They are also the questions worth asking before you accept flexible work, including work we discuss with you. The report is about teenagers in retail, food and beverage, and logistics. The standard travels. The jobs in that video are not the jobs on this page.

01

Ask for income and a skill

Ma told CNA the younger jobseekers wanted to earn, and they wanted an early start. Pay matters. A skill you can name matters beside it. In a Pogo Hire conversation, ask which habit the role uses each week: checking data, writing, talking with customers, research, or keeping a schedule.

02

Prefer a stint you can mix with the rest of your week

FastGig lets students combine short roles. Tan said the youngest jobseekers were looking for variety, where a holiday used to mean one place every day. Bring your real week. Holiday weeks and ordinary weeks are different. You should be able to see the length of a stint before you accept it, and to decline hours that do not fit.

03

Treat communication as the work skill

Rico Ng, a 17-year-old diploma student on shifts at Chagee, told CNA that busy hours depend on teamwork and clear communication, and on being respectful with customers. He also said juggling school projects and shifts was practice in time management. Customer-facing digital work asks for those same habits. Rico is not a Pogo Hire client, and we do not hire for Chagee.

04

Look for real responsibility

Chagee calls full-time and part-time staff partners, and says teenagers are given real responsibilities. The useful question is the same at any age: what will you own, who shows you the task, and what does finished work look like?

05

Keep the word “may” in front of any head start

CNA closed on a careful line: beyond pocket money, short stints may give young workers a head start. A few weeks can show how you communicate and how you manage time. That experience does not promise a permanent job, a higher salary, or a particular career result. We will describe a role in those terms.

“They want to earn income, but they also want an early start to workplace experience and exposure.” Jeremy Ma, head of operations, Chagee Singapore, quoted by CNA on 30 December 2025. This is a news quote, not a testimonial for Pogo Hire.

Hook 01 · A skill you can name

Digital operations assistant

Daily digital workflows and data entry for a structured brief. The skill on the table is accuracy and a steady routine.

Hook 04 · A task you own

HR data coordinator

Document checks and employee-data updates. The task has a definition of done: the record is complete, or it is sent back with a reason.

Hook 03 · Communication

Customer relations

Inbound questions by live chat. The practice is clear replies, patience, and a professional tone when the queue is busy.

Hook 01 · Judgement

Content review

Reading digital content against a written guideline. The skill is attention, not speed for its own sake.

Hook 02 · A bounded stint

Research support

Online research gathered into a short brief. Hours need focus, and the piece of work has an end.

Hook 02 · A week you can see

Admin support

Scheduling and digital records. Useful when you want a predictable block of hours rather than a shifting queue.

These are examples of the kind of work a conversation can cover. They are not live vacancies, and they do not show pay. Hours, length, duties, and pay are confirmed in writing before you accept anything. Amounts differ by engagement.

The conversation

A person reads your note

We do not publish a match score. A consultant reviews what you send and replies when there is a possible fit. The team aims to reply within 1–2 business days. That aim is not a guarantee of a reply, a match, or an offer.

Straight answers

Is this an application for a job that is open today?

No. It is a request for a conversation. The role examples describe the kind of digital work we discuss. Availability changes, and many weeks there is nothing that fits.

Are you the app or the tea chain in the video?

No. FastCo, FastGig, FastJobs, and Chagee belong to their own operators. We cite CNA’s report because the five points are a sound test for flexible work. They have not endorsed Pogo Hire.

Why mention teenagers if the offer starts at 25?

Because that report is the clearest recent public account of what people want from a short stint: pay, practice, variety, real tasks, and no inflated promise. We apply the standard to our own audience. We do not recruit under-25s with this page.

Can I do this around another commitment?

Yes, if the hours you offer are hours you can keep. Say what your week already contains. A stint that ignores that week fails the second test.
